I taught Combustion Processes (AER315) as the sole course instructor at the University of Toronto in Fall 2020, the first time the course was offered online, preparing my own slides and course material for remote delivery. I have also been a teaching assistant for undergraduate and graduate courses at the University of Toronto and IIT Bombay (2017–2023). The course page below includes the weekly schedule and my lecture handouts.

#### Teaching approach

My goal in teaching is to build critical thinking together with technical skill: for every method, students should ask when to use it, why it works, where it fails and what follows from it. I start each module from a real system, such as a candle flame, and simplify it until the underlying physics is visible; I plan each lecture around a clear learning outcome; I keep a class of mixed preparation engaged with visual media, simulations and experimental evidence; and I match assessment to the class, with semester-long projects in smaller graduate courses and frequent short quizzes and computational assignments (Python, MATLAB, open-source solvers) in large undergraduate classes.

#### Courses I can teach

- **Core:** thermodynamics, heat and mass transfer, fluid mechanics, energy systems, mechanical measurements and laboratories.
- **Electives:** combustion, aircraft and rocket propulsion, aerosol science.
- **New courses I would like to develop:**
  - _Experimental Methods and Optical Diagnostics in Thermal-Fluid Systems_: a hands-on course on laser and optical methods (absorption, emission, scattering and laser-induced incandescence) and data reduction for reacting and multiphase flows.
  - _Combustion Emissions, Sustainable Fuels and Climate Impact_: soot and pollutant formation; combustion of hydrogen, ammonia and sustainable aviation fuels; air-quality and climate effects, including contrails.
  - _Radiative Heat Transfer in Participating Media_: radiative properties of gases and particles and solution methods, including Monte Carlo ray tracing, with applications to flames, combustors and atmospheric aerosols.
