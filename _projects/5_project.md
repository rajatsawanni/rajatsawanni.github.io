### Overview

Sustainable aviation fuels (SAF) and hydrogen can cut the life-cycle CO<sub>2</sub> of aviation, but they also change how soot forms, and soot both harms air quality and seeds contrails. These ongoing postdoctoral projects at UTIAS measure how fuel composition and hydrogen addition change soot formation under engine-relevant conditions.

---

## Emission characterization of SAF in a miniature gas-turbine model combustor

_Principal Investigator: Prof. Ömer L. Gülder_

I am contributing to the development of a **1 kW miniature gas-turbine model combustor** that generates gas-turbine-like soot while consuming only small volumes of fuel. This makes it possible to test novel fuels such as SAF and oxygenated hydrocarbons, which are often available only in small quantities, and to measure their volatile and non-volatile particulate emissions.

## Hydrogen addition and soot formation at high pressure

_Principal Investigator: Prof. Ömer L. Gülder, in collaboration with Prof. Emre Karataş_

Using the high-pressure counterflow burner I built during my Ph.D., this project isolates the **chemical effect of hydrogen addition** on the sooting tendency of ethylene counterflow diffusion flames at elevated pressure. The aim is to understand the role of hydrogen in the sooting tendencies of hydrocarbon fuels at high pressure, which matters for hydrogen-enriched gas turbines. I mentor a doctoral candidate from Toronto Metropolitan University on this work.
