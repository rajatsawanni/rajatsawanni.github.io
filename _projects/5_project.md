Sustainable aviation fuels (SAF) and hydrogen can cut aviation's life-cycle CO<sub>2</sub>, but they also change how soot forms, and soot matters both for air quality and because it seeds contrails. These are two projects I'm working on now as a postdoc at UTIAS, with Prof. Ömer L. Gülder.

## A small gas-turbine combustor for testing new fuels

New fuels are often only available in small quantities, which makes engine-scale testing hard. I'm helping develop a **1 kW miniature gas-turbine model combustor** that produces gas-turbine-like soot from only a little fuel, so we can test fuels such as SAF and oxygenated hydrocarbons and measure their volatile and non-volatile particulate emissions.

## What hydrogen does to soot at high pressure

Using the [high-pressure counterflow burner](/projects/2_project/) I built during my Ph.D., this project, in collaboration with Prof. Emre Karataş, isolates the **chemical effect of adding hydrogen** to ethylene flames at high pressure. Hydrogen-enriched gas turbines are one motivation, and the open question is how hydrogen changes the way hydrocarbon fuels make soot when the pressure goes up. I'm also mentoring a Ph.D. student from Toronto Metropolitan University on this work.
